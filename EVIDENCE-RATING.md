SIGNAL 01 — EVIDENCE RATING SYSTEM

Version: 1.0
Project: SIGNAL 01

⸻

PURPOSE

This document defines how potential responses to SIGNAL 01 will be evaluated.

The classification criteria are established in advance.

They must not be changed simply because a particular result appears unusual or convincing.

⸻

LEVEL 0 — COINCIDENCE

The event contains no independently verifiable information.

Examples:

* vague statements;
* generic messages;
* random numbers;
* common predictions;
* patterns discovered only after the event;
* information already publicly available.

Assessment: no evidence of unknown intelligence.

⸻

LEVEL 1 — UNUSUAL PATTERN

The event contains an unusual or apparently structured pattern.

However, plausible explanations remain available.

Examples:

* unexpected numerical patterns;
* unusual timing;
* apparent linguistic structures;
* coincidences involving public information.

Assessment: interesting, but insufficient.

⸻

LEVEL 2 — NON-TRIVIAL INFORMATION

The event contains specific information that appears difficult to explain through ordinary coincidence.

The information must have been unavailable to the investigators before the event.

However, independent confirmation is still incomplete.

Assessment: requires further investigation.

⸻

LEVEL 3 — REPLICABLE ANOMALY

The phenomenon produces new, testable information repeatedly.

Independent investigators can reproduce the result.

The phenomenon cannot be adequately explained by:

* known human sources;
* publicly available information;
* technical artifacts;
* ordinary statistical effects;
* deliberate manipulation;
* or simple coincidence.

Assessment: strong anomaly requiring serious investigation.

⸻

LEVEL 4 — EXTRAORDINARY EVIDENCE

A result reaches this level only if:

1. the original data are preserved;
2. independent investigators verify the observation;
3. the information was genuinely unavailable beforehand;
4. predictions are specific and successfully verified;
5. the result is reproducible;
6. major alternative explanations have been investigated;
7. independent teams reach substantially the same conclusion.

Assessment: extraordinary evidence requiring broad scientific scrutiny.

⸻

INFORMATION NOVELTY TEST

For every potential response, ask:

Was the information already publicly available?

If yes, its evidentiary value is greatly reduced.

Was the information known to any participant?

If yes, this must be disclosed.

Was the prediction recorded before verification?

If no, it cannot be treated as a strong prediction.

Can another person reproduce the test?

If no, the evidence remains weak.

⸻

PREDICTION SCORE

A prediction becomes stronger when it is:

Specific
rather than vague.

Time-limited
rather than open-ended.

Recorded beforehand
rather than reconstructed afterward.

Independent
rather than dependent on the original observer.

Unlikely by chance
rather than a common event.

⸻

NEGATIVE RESULTS

Negative results are valid results.

If a supposed signal is later explained by a normal phenomenon, the project will document the explanation.

The purpose is investigation, not confirmation of a predetermined belief.

⸻

FINAL RULE

An unexplained event is not automatically an unknown intelligence.

A strange pattern is not automatically a message.

A message is not automatically evidence.

Evidence must survive attempts to disprove it.
