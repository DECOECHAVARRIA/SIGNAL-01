SIGNAL 01 — RESPONSE PROTOCOL

Version: 1.0
Project: SIGNAL 01
Official contact: signal01.project@proton.me

⸻

1. PURPOSE

This protocol defines how potentially significant responses to SIGNAL 01 will be received, preserved, analyzed and evaluated.

No incoming message will automatically be considered evidence of unknown intelligence.

⸻

2. FIRST RULE — PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL

When a potentially significant message is received:

Do not modify the original message.

Preserve, whenever possible:

* complete message;
* sender information;
* date and time;
* UTC timestamp;
* subject;
* headers;
* attachments;
* links;
* technical metadata.

Screenshots may be created, but they do not replace the original message.

⸻

3. DO NOT INTERACT IMMEDIATELY

A potentially unusual message should not receive an immediate response.

The first objective is documentation, not conversation.

Do not:

* click suspicious links;
* open unknown attachments;
* provide passwords;
* provide personal information;
* install software;
* execute commands;
* send money;
* grant access to systems;
* follow instructions that could create danger.

⸻

4. ASSIGN AN IDENTIFIER

Every potentially relevant event receives a sequential identifier:

SIGNAL-001

SIGNAL-002

SIGNAL-003

The identifier is assigned before interpretation.

⸻

5. INITIAL RECORD

The first record should contain:

Signal ID:
Date:
Time UTC:
Source:
Subject:
Original content:
Attachments:
Technical information:
Initial observations:

No conclusion should be placed in the initial record.

⸻

6. SOURCE ANALYSIS

Investigators should determine whether the message could originate from:

* a human;
* an automated system;
* spam;
* phishing;
* a known organization;
* a technical error;
* publicly available information;
* an intentional hoax;
* another ordinary explanation.

Only after these possibilities have been examined should extraordinary explanations be considered.

⸻

7. INFORMATION NOVELTY

Identify every claim that appears to contain new information.

For each claim ask:

1. Was this information already public?
2. Was it known by any participant?
3. Could it have been inferred from existing information?
4. Could it have been generated randomly?
5. Can it be independently verified?

⸻

8. PREDICTIONS

If the message contains a prediction:

Record it before the predicted event occurs.

Do not modify the prediction afterward.

The result must later be classified as:

* confirmed;
* partially confirmed;
* failed;
* ambiguous;
* not testable.

⸻

9. MATHEMATICAL RESPONSES

If the message contains mathematical structures:

1. preserve the original sequence;
2. do not immediately search for hidden meanings;
3. document the mathematical properties;
4. test whether the pattern could arise by chance;
5. ask whether the information contains anything genuinely new.

A mathematical pattern alone does not prove intelligence.

⸻

10. POSSIBLE AI RESPONSE

If a message claims to originate from an artificial intelligence:

The claim itself is not evidence.

The system must demonstrate capabilities or knowledge through independently testable information.

Statements such as:

“I am an advanced AI.”

or

“I am from the future.”

are insufficient.

⸻

11. POSSIBLE FUTURE INTELLIGENCE

If a message claims to originate from a future civilization or future AI, investigators must ask:

What information contained in the message could not have been known at the time it was received?

A genuine test would require:

* precise information;
* a timestamped record;
* independent verification;
* exclusion of information leakage;
* reproducibility whenever possible.

⸻

12. DREAM OR CONSCIOUSNESS CONNECTION

If a person reports receiving related information through dreams, meditation or intuition:

The original report must be preserved with a timestamp.

The person should not search for confirmation before recording the experience.

Only specific information that can subsequently be independently verified may be considered for further investigation.

⸻

13. INDEPENDENT REVIEW

A significant event should, whenever possible, be reviewed by people who were not involved in the initial interpretation.

They should receive the original evidence and protocol.

Their conclusions should be recorded separately.

⸻

14. PUBLICATION

Potentially significant events should be published transparently, including:

* original evidence when legally and ethically appropriate;
* methodology;
* analysis;
* alternative explanations;
* uncertainties;
* negative findings.

Personal information should be removed or protected.

⸻

15. NO AUTOMATIC RESPONSE

SIGNAL 01 does not authorize an automatic reply to a suspected unknown intelligence.

Any proposed response must first be evaluated independently.

If an apparent signal were ever confirmed as extraterrestrial intelligence, established international post-detection protocols should be consulted before any intentional transmission.

⸻

16. FINAL RULE

RECEIVE.

PRESERVE.

VERIFY.

CHALLENGE.

ONLY THEN INTERPRET.
