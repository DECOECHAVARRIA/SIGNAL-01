SIGNAL 01 — DREAM & CONSCIOUSNESS REPORTS

Version: 1.0
Project: SIGNAL 01

⸻

PURPOSE

This document provides a standardized method for recording dreams, meditation experiences, intuition, unusual perceptions or other subjective experiences that participants believe may contain information related to SIGNAL 01.

These experiences are treated as observations to be documented, not as proof of communication.

⸻

1. IMMEDIATE RECORD

A participant should record the experience as soon as possible.

The original record should contain:

Report ID:
Date:
Time:
Location:
State of consciousness:
Original description:
Specific information perceived:

The participant should avoid searching the internet for the information before creating the original record.

⸻

2. SPECIFICITY

A useful report should contain specific information whenever possible.

Weak example:

“I felt that something was trying to communicate.”

Strong example:

“I perceived the number 7319 and recorded it immediately after waking.”

Specific information allows an independent test.

⸻

3. TIMESTAMP

Whenever possible, the original report should have a reliable timestamp.

Examples include:

* email timestamp;
* version-controlled file;
* photograph with metadata;
* independent witness;
* secure digital record.

The objective is to establish that the information existed before verification.

⸻

4. INFORMATION LEAKAGE

Before evaluating a report, investigators should determine whether the participant could have encountered the information previously.

Possible sources include:

* websites;
* social media;
* conversations;
* books;
* videos;
* news;
* previous SIGNAL 01 discussions;
* subconscious exposure to information.

Known exposure does not invalidate the personal experience, but it reduces its value as evidence of an external source.

⸻

5. BLIND VERIFICATION

Whenever practical, verification should be performed without telling the evaluator the expected result.

This reduces confirmation bias.

For example, if a participant records a numerical prediction, an independent person should evaluate whether the prediction was fulfilled without being encouraged to find a match.

⸻

6. PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS

If a report contains a prediction:

1. record the exact prediction;
2. timestamp the record;
3. define the verification criteria;
4. wait for the relevant event;
5. compare the result with the original record.

Do not modify the prediction afterward.

⸻

7. REPEATED EVENTS

A single unusual experience is insufficient to establish communication.

Repeated observations are more informative when:

* they are independently recorded;
* they contain specific information;
* they occur before verification;
* they produce measurable results;
* alternative explanations are considered.

⸻

8. NEGATIVE RESULTS

Reports that do not produce successful predictions should remain in the archive when relevant.

Failure is part of the experiment.

⸻

9. PRIVACY

Participants should not publish:

* passwords;
* private addresses;
* financial information;
* medical information;
* personal identification documents;
* private communications belonging to other people.

Sensitive personal information should be removed before publication.

⸻

10. INTERPRETATION

SIGNAL 01 does not determine the cause of a dream, intuition or subjective experience.

Possible explanations include:

* normal cognitive processes;
* memory;
* coincidence;
* unconscious pattern recognition;
* prior exposure;
* expectation;
* external information;
* unknown mechanisms.

The project records the observation first and investigates possible explanations afterward.

⸻

FINAL PRINCIPLE

RECORD FIRST.

VERIFY SECOND.

INTERPRET LAST.

A subjective experience may be meaningful to the person experiencing it.

Scientific significance, however, requires independent verification.
